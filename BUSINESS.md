# SecureStart

**One-line pitch:** SecureStart helps South African small professional firms find their cybersecurity and POPIA gaps, turn each gap into a tracked fix, and show an owner or regulator exactly how their security is improving.

---

## 1. The problem

Small firms such as accounting practices, law firms and medical or dental practices hold client personal information, but they have no security team and no simple way to know where they stand.

- **Who has it:** firms of roughly 5 to 50 staff that process personal information (client files, IDs, financial and health records) and rely on an outsourced IT provider or one "IT person".
- **How often:** every day. Phishing, weak passwords, unpatched software and misdirected emails are routine, not rare events.
- **What it costs today:**
  - Under POPIA, every security compromise has to be reported to the Information Regulator and affected people, and there is no minimum size for a reportable incident. The Regulator's October 2026 statement fined Central Johannesburg TVET College R500,000 after an accidental internal disclosure that it did not report properly and a notice it did not comply with.
  - At its 31 August 2026 briefing, the Regulator said it had received over 8,000 security compromise notifications since its enforcement powers began, and that it is moving from awareness into enforcement.
  - Failing to comply with an enforcement notice is a criminal offence, with fines of up to R10 million, up to ten years' imprisonment, or both.
  - Industry commentary (vendor-sponsored, so treat with care) puts SA cyberattack losses at an estimated R2.2 billion in 2025 and says only about 19% of SA firms are adequately prepared.
- **Today's workaround:** a spreadsheet checklist, an occasional consultant visit, or nothing. Nobody tracks whether the fixes actually happen.

> Sources to verify and cite before submission: Werksmans (31 Aug 2026 briefing summary; 30 Oct 2026 TVET fine article), the Information Regulator's own statements (inforegulator.org.za), BusinessTech/Axxess (19 Aug 2026), Kaspersky SMB research for South Africa.

## 2. The customer

- **User:** the firm's owner or practice manager, who needs a plain-language view of risk.
- **Security administrator:** the person who does the technical work. For most small firms this is an independent IT consultant or small managed-service provider (MSP), not an in-house employee.
- **Who pays:** the firm subscribes (Starter). Consultants and MSPs pay for the Business tier because it lets them manage several client firms from one account.

## 3. The solution

SecureStart is a guided assessment and remediation tracker. A firm answers a structured questionnaire built on a recognised baseline (CIS Controls v8, Implementation Group 1, mapped to POPIA security safeguard requirements). Every "no" or "partly" answer automatically becomes a prioritised risk item with an owner, a due date and a status. The dashboard shows a transparent score and how it changes as items are fixed.

**What it is not:** it does not scan systems or run automated penetration tests, and it does not give legal advice. It organises, prioritises and tracks the work, and it shows exactly how each score is calculated.

## 4. The impact

What changes if SecureStart succeeds: a small firm goes from "we think we're fine" to a documented, improving security position it can show clients and the Regulator.

How we measure it:
- Average score improvement between a firm's first and latest assessment.
- Share of high-priority risks resolved within 30 days.
- Number of firms with documented incident-response and information-officer basics in place.
- Time from finding a gap to fixing it.

## 5. Business model

| Plan | Who | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Any firm | One basic assessment and a score |
| Starter | Single firm | Full assessment, risk register, remediation tracking, dashboard |
| Business | Consultants/MSPs and larger firms | Multiple client firms and users, exportable reports, ongoing risk tracking |

Price points are hypotheses to validate with real customers: Starter around R499 per month and Business around R1,499 per month. The free assessment is the lead magnet; the paid plans are about keeping the tracking going.

## 6. Go-to-market: first 100 customers

1. **Consultants and MSPs first (channel):** each one manages 5 to 20 small firms. Ten partners can bring in the first 50 to 100 firms.
2. **Free assessment as a lead magnet:** shared in professional circles for accountants, lawyers and practice managers, with a POPIA-readiness angle.
3. **Content:** plain-language writeups on POPIA enforcement and common small-firm security gaps, published on Medium and LinkedIn from the South African security and CTF community.
4. **Local business groups:** chambers of commerce, practice-management groups and short workshops.
5. **Referral loop:** a consultant who finishes a client assessment gets a shareable report that points other firms to the free assessment.
